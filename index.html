Privacy Policy for Brainigo

Last updated: 21 November 2026

Introduction

Brainigo ("we", "our", or "the app") helps you learn piano, guitar, bass, drums, singing and other instruments by playing short drills that are scored note by note. It runs on the web and as an app for Android, iPhone and iPad. Brainigo is made by Digitalix.

This policy explains what the app collects, what leaves your device, and who else processes it. We have tried to be specific rather than reassuring: where your data does leave your device, this policy says when and to whom.

Summary

  • The microphone is analysed on your device and never recorded. No audio of your playing or singing is stored or sent anywhere.
  • You do not need an account. Without one, your progress stays on your device and nothing about you is sent to us.
  • If you sign in, your learners, practice results and settings sync to our servers so they follow you to other devices, and so you can work with a tutor.
  • If you ask the AI coach a question, your question and a summary of your scores are sent to an AI provider to write the answer. Never audio, never names.
  • Profiles marked Under 13 get extra protection: the AI coach is off unless the account holder turns it on, and no usage statistics are sent while a child is playing.
  • We do not sell your data, show advertising, or use advertising trackers.

Data stored on your device

Held in your browser’s storage or the app’s own storage, and never uploaded unless a section below says otherwise:

  • Your settings: instrument, sounds, display, timing and latency calibration, and microphone choices
  • Learner profiles on this device: the name, picture and colour you choose, and whether a profile is for a child under 13
  • Progress: which lessons and drills you have played, your scores and stars, streaks, practice minutes and review schedule
  • Drills you build and save, and what the coach has already suggested for each drill
  • If you sign in, the sign-in session that keeps you signed in

You can delete all of it by clearing the site’s data in your browser, clearing the app’s storage, or uninstalling the app.

Your microphone, keyboard and other devices

Microphone

When you choose to play or sing into the microphone, Brainigo listens to work out which notes you played and when. This happens on your device, as you play: the sound is turned into note and timing information and then discarded. It is never recorded, stored or uploaded. The app only listens while a drill or tuner that uses the microphone is open and you have turned the microphone on.

Spoken commands

In the phone apps you can say a few words, such as “start”, “stop” or “slower”, instead of touching the screen. They are recognised by your phone’s own speech recognition on the device: the app asks only for on-device recognition and does not fall back to an online service. If your phone cannot recognise speech on the device, spoken commands are simply not available.

MIDI keyboards, electronic drum kits and Bluetooth

If you connect a keyboard or drum kit, by cable or Bluetooth, the app reads the notes it sends in order to score your playing. Bluetooth permission is used only to connect to such an instrument.

What a scored run contains

Whichever way you play, a scored run of a drill is a list of notes: which note, when, and how hard. If you are signed in, that list is what syncs (see below). It is not a recording and cannot be played back as your sound.

Data that leaves your device

1. Your account — only if you sign in

Signing in is optional. You sign in through Clerk, our authentication provider, which receives the email address and name you sign in with (and, if you use a social login such as Google, the profile details it shares) and keeps your sign-in session. On the phone apps, sign-in opens in your phone’s browser.

Once you are signed in, the following is stored on our backend (Convex):

  • Your account: an identifier from Clerk, your name and email address, your time zone (for the dates in emails), and which kinds of email you have turned off
  • The learners on your account: for each, the name, picture and colour you chose, whether they are under 13, and whether the AI coach may answer them
  • Practice results: for each scored run, the drill, the tempo, the scores and stars, your latency setting, and the list of notes you played with their timing and loudness (no audio)
  • A summary per drill (best score and tempo, how many runs) and how many minutes you practised each day
  • Your practice reminder time and daily goal
  • Drills you build and save to your account
  • How many questions you asked the AI coach each day (to apply the daily allowance)

2. Working with a tutor

Brainigo lets music teachers (tutors) set homework, book lessons and message their students. If you use these features, more is shared, with the people involved:

  • If you join a tutor (with their invite code or by booking a lesson), that tutor sees the learner’s name, the homework they set and how it is going, and the learner’s recent scored runs (drill, date and scores).
  • Lessons: when you book, the tutor sees the learner’s name, the time, whether it is a free first lesson, and any message you add. Tutors can write notes on a lesson for the learner.
  • Messages between a tutor and a learner are stored on our servers so both of you can read them on any device.
  • Reviews you write about a lesson are shown on the tutor’s public listing with the learner’s name and are used in the tutor’s rating.
  • Waitlists: the tutor sees the learner’s name when you join their waitlist.
  • Video lessons: where a lesson takes place in Brainigo, the call’s audio and video are carried between tutor and learner by LiveKit. We do not record calls. A tutor may instead give a link to their own video service, which then handles the call under its own policy.
  • Blocking: you can block the other person from a conversation. We store that you blocked them, so that neither of you can write and the learner cannot book that tutor. Blocking does not remove reviews.
  • Reports: you can report a message, a review or a tutor’s listing. We store who reported it, the reason, any note you add, and a copy of the words reported, and email them to the person at Brainigo who reviews reports. The person reported is not told who reported them.
  • Payment for lessons is arranged between you and the tutor directly. Brainigo does not process payments or receive card details.

If you are a tutor, your listing (display name, headline, bio, instruments, levels and ages taught, languages, price, lesson length, time zone, cancellation terms, and ratings and reviews) is public in Find a tutor once you choose to list it. Your video-call link is shown only to learners who have booked. Your students’ data is theirs: use it only to teach them.

3. The AI coach

The coach answers many questions inside the app without sending anything. When a question needs the AI coach (you must be signed in), our backend sends the AI provider:

  • your question and the recent turns of that conversation with the coach
  • a summary from the scoring engine: the drill, your recent scores, the bars that keep going wrong, and how much help you have had

It does not send audio, your name, your email or anything else about you. Today the reply is written by Google Gemini, and the question and reply are checked by TypeSafe (to keep the conversation on music practice and suitable for children). We may change providers; this page will name the ones in use.

For a profile marked Under 13 the AI coach is off unless the account holder turns it on for that profile.

4. Email

If you have an account, we may email you: a welcome, a weekly progress report, lesson bookings, cancellations and reminders, homework and messages from your tutor, and account notices (such as confirming that your account was deleted). Every email that is not about your account has an unsubscribe link, and you can turn categories off in Settings. Emails are sent through an email delivery provider. A copy of each email is kept for up to 30 days so it can be retried if sending fails; its contents are cleared once it has been sent. If an address bounces or reports our mail as spam, we keep that address on a do-not-send list.

5. Notifications

On the web, if you allow notifications, your browser gives us a push address for this device, which we store with your account. Practice reminders and lesson notices are then sent through your browser’s push service (for example Google’s, Mozilla’s or Apple’s), encrypted so that only your browser can read them.

In the phone apps, practice reminders are scheduled on your phone itself.

Inside the app, a list of notices (bookings, cancellations, homework, notes and messages) is stored with your account.

6. Usage statistics (web only)

Brainigo on the web sends usage events to Amplitude to help us see which parts of the app work: for example that a drill was started, completed or abandoned, a lesson or unit was finished, a daily goal was met, a device was connected, or a setting changed (which setting, not your choices). Events are tied to a random identifier for that browser, not to your account, name or email.

  • No events are sent while a profile marked Under 13 is playing.
  • The phone apps do not send usage statistics.
  • You can stop it by blocking amplitude.com in your browser or with a content blocker; the app works the same.

7. Shared drills

When you share a drill, the whole drill is packed into the link itself. It does not pass through our servers; it goes only where you send it.

Children

A Brainigo account is for someone aged 13 or over (or older, where the law where you live requires it). A child under 13 can learn on a profile that a parent or guardian adds to their own account and marks Under 13. For those profiles:

  • the AI coach is off unless the account holder turns it on;
  • no usage statistics are sent while the child is playing;
  • we ask for no information about the child beyond a name, which can be a nickname;
  • anything a tutor sees, and any messages with a tutor, belong to the parent’s account, which can remove the profile or delete everything at any time.

If you believe a child under 13 has an account of their own, write to us and we will delete it.

How long we keep data

  • On your device: until you clear it or uninstall the app.
  • Your account and everything in it: while you have an account, until you delete it.
  • Emails: up to 30 days, with the contents cleared once sent. The do-not-send list is kept so that we keep respecting it.
  • AI coach daily counts: removed after a week.
  • Reports and blocks: kept while both accounts exist, so that past reports can inform later decisions; removed when either account is deleted.
  • Usage statistics: kept by Amplitude under its retention settings, tied only to a random browser identifier.

Deleting your account

Delete your account yourself in the Brainigo app under Settings → Your account → Delete account, or on the web under Settings → Account & sync → Delete account. That removes every learner on it, their results, homework, lessons and messages, your tutor page if you teach, and your sign-in. Upcoming lessons are cancelled and the other person is told; past lessons stay in a tutor’s history as “Deleted account”. We send one last email to confirm. Delete your account explains every way to do it, including without the app.

Security

Everything sent between the app and our services is encrypted in transit (HTTPS). Only you can read your account’s data, and only the people you work with (such as your tutor) see the parts this policy says they see.

Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to access, correct, export or delete your personal data, to object to or restrict how we use it, and to complain to your data protection authority. You can correct and delete most of it in the app. For anything else, write to us at the address below and we will reply within 30 days.

Changes to this policy

We will update this page when the app’s data handling changes, and revise the "Last updated" date above. If a change is significant, we will tell you in the app or by email before it applies.
